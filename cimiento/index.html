Pick: v5 — Compare Room

Job: pick an area → see the ranked place × business bets (rent, sale, gross-yield sketch, demand gap, confidence) and act on them right there. Audience: investors on a laptop, analytical, skeptical of black boxes.

Why v5

  1. It answers the job's literal question, not just "here is a yield". The rent × occupancy sensitivity grid plus the break-even line — "at the expected rent ($22), the yield drops below your 7.0% target when occupancy falls under 79%; our estimate: 91%" — is exactly "will demand hold occupancy and rent?", stated against the investor's own target.
  2. Nothing is a black box. Every assumption (target yield, leasable area, purchase costs, per-bet rent and occupancy) is visible and editable, the grid shows a range instead of a point estimate, and confidence and missing data sit as rows in the same table as the yield — a skeptic can audit a bet without leaving the screen.
  3. Built for the laptop compare scene. Four bets as columns with the best in each row marked, area tabs that start a scan in one click, and the whole result (matrix + sensitivity grid + map) above the fold at 1440.

The move to English does not change the judgement: the copy got shorter (the headline now fits in two lines at 1440), and the condensed Encode Sans keeps the dense, tabular feel the Hebrew version had.

Borrow from the runner-up (v1, Assumptions Sheet)

The spreadsheet convention: blue = your input, black = calculated, and an fx line under the matrix that spells out the selected bet's yield formula term by term. It makes the editable cells read as inputs at a glance and gives the formula a fixed home.

Known weak spots of v5

  • On a phone the matrix scrolls sideways (sticky row labels); acceptable for this audience, but the phone's first screen is still mostly header (tabs wrap to two rows).
  • The map is a small strip under the sensitivity grid and falls below the fold at 1440.
