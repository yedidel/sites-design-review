Terna home screen — Claude's pick: v1 "The Client Table"

Job: in five seconds a broker sees that Terna is a client workspace (criteria → a shortlist of three sites → a live compare that includes overlap with the client's own branches → a decision), and can start a new client from that same screen. Brokers use it on a laptop, often while screen-sharing with the client.

Why v1

  1. The whole workflow is on one screen, and every step can be acted on. The stepper (Criteria → Shortlist → Compare → Decision), the criteria chips, the three site cards, the overlap map and the recommendation box all fit in the first 1440 px view. A broker sees how the work is done instead of reading a pitch about it.
  2. The shortlist behaves the way the meeting does. The three cards are columns lined up on factor rows, with the best value in each row marked. The broker drags the cards into presenting order. Criterion toggles flag any site that breaks the brief, and the recommendation sentence rewrites itself, all live while the client watches.
  3. It reads as a working product a broker would trust on a shared screen. It uses standard controls, a calm bottle-green shell and dense but readable tabular figures (IBM Plex Sans). It passes the category test: it looks as clean as Placer.ai or SiteZeus. With the logo covered it still couldn't be a sibling site, because the client rail and the side-by-side shortlist exist only here.

The English rewrite doesn't change this judgement. The copy fits v1's fixed-height rows. The labels are short ("Fit score", "Daytime workers (10-min walk)") and switch to one word on the phone. The stepper still reads naturally left to right. v2's one-sentence brief gained the most from English, but it is still a persuade-first screen, not a workspace.

Borrow from the runner-up (v4 "The Decision Memo")

Take v4's "Present to client" switch. v1 shows other clients' names in the rail and has room for internal notes, and a broker should never screen-share those. One switch should hide the rail and the broker-only notes and enlarge the compare area. With that, v1 is safe to share and is still a full workspace.

A close third is v3's walk-time control (5/10/15 min), which recomputes the overlap percentage live. It is the strongest proof of the cannibalisation claim and could go into v1's map panel later.
